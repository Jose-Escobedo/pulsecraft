"How much should this cost?" is the wrong first question — it's the one that gets business owners locked into a $12/month template that quietly costs them leads for years. The better question is what you're actually paying for at each price point, because the range is enormous and the reasons for it aren't always obvious.

## The DIY builder tier ($0–$30/month)

Wix, Squarespace, and similar platforms are genuinely fine for a hobby project or a placeholder page. What you're trading for the low price: generic templates that dozens of competitors in your market are also using, bloated page-builder code that hurts load speed, and limited control over the technical SEO fundamentals that actually move rankings.

For a business depending on its website for leads, this tier's real cost shows up later — in traffic that doesn't convert.

## The freelancer tier ($500–$3,000 one-time)

Wide quality range here. A good freelancer can deliver real custom work at a fair price; a bad one delivers a slightly customized template with no ongoing support. The differentiator is almost never the price — it's whether they're building on solid technical foundations (fast hosting, clean code, real SEO structure) or just making something that looks right in a screenshot.

## The agency tier ($3,000–$25,000+)

This is where you're paying for process: discovery, strategy, custom design, and (ideally) a technical foundation built for performance and search from day one, not retrofitted later. The wide range within this tier usually comes down to scope — a 5-page marketing site versus a custom web application are not the same project, even though both get called "a website."

## What you should actually be pricing

Regardless of tier, the real cost drivers are:

- **Number of custom pages** vs. templated ones
- **Integrations** — booking systems, payment processing, CRM, IDX/MLS feeds, and similar
- **Ongoing hosting, maintenance, and updates** — often excluded from the upfront price and billed separately later
- **SEO foundation** — whether the site is built to actually rank, or just built to exist

Not "how much," but "what happens to my site speed and my search ranking with this build?" A $99/mo hand-coded site and a $12/mo template can look similar in a screenshot and perform completely differently in Google — and performance is the part that keeps paying you back after launch.
