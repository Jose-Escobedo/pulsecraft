Search "[your service] near me" in Los Angeles and three businesses show up above every organic result, with a map, reviews, and a phone number front and center. That's the local 3-pack — and for most local businesses, it drives more calls than the entire rest of page one combined.

If you're not in it for your core searches, here's what's actually influencing that ranking, roughly in order of impact.

## 1. Your Google Business Profile is the foundation

Before your website matters at all, Google is reading your Business Profile: category selection, business name, service area, hours, and — critically — how closely your name, address, and phone number (NAP) match what's listed everywhere else about your business online, including your own website.

A mismatch between your GBP listing and your website's footer or Contact page (a different phone number, an inconsistent business name, a service area that doesn't match) is one of the most common reasons an otherwise strong business quietly underperforms locally.

## 2. Reviews — volume, recency, and response

The 3-pack algorithm weighs review count, average rating, and how recently reviews came in. Businesses that ask every satisfied customer for a review, and actually respond to reviews (especially negative ones), consistently outrank businesses with a handful of reviews from three years ago — even with an otherwise stronger website.

## 3. Proximity — the one thing you can't fully control

Google factors in how close a searcher is to your service area. This is why the 3-pack often looks different for the same search from Santa Monica versus Downtown LA. You can't move your business, but you can make sure your service area is configured correctly in GBP and reflected accurately in your [areas served on your website](/web-design) — vague or missing service-area information leaves Google guessing.

## 4. On-page relevance — where your website comes back in

This is the piece most businesses get wrong: a single homepage trying to represent every service and every neighborhood dilutes relevance instead of building it. A [dedicated page per city](/web-design) and per service consistently outperforms one generic page trying to rank for everything at once, because it gives Google a specific, well-matched page to serve for each specific search.

Site speed factors in here too, more directly than most business owners expect — Google has been explicit that page experience, including Core Web Vitals, is a ranking input, and a slow site undermines the relevance signals you've otherwise built.

## 5. Local structured data

Beyond the visible page, `LocalBusiness` schema markup gives Google a machine-readable version of your NAP and service area, reinforcing (not replacing) what's in your Business Profile. It's a smaller signal than reviews or proximity, but it's also nearly free to implement correctly and costs you nothing to get right.

## Where to start

If you're only going to fix one thing this month, fix the mismatch first: pull up your Google Business Profile side by side with your website's footer and Contact page, and make sure the name, phone number, and service area say exactly the same thing in both places. Everything else compounds on top of that foundation.
