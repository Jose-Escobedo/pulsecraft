Most real estate websites are built to impress at a listing presentation, not to generate leads at 11pm when a buyer is scrolling Zillow, gets curious about the agent, and clicks through. That second moment is where most agent sites quietly lose the lead.

## Your IDX feed shouldn't be the whole strategy

A plugin-based IDX integration is table stakes, not a differentiator — every agent using the same MLS provider often ends up with the same generic listing template. It also tends to be the single biggest drag on site speed, since most IDX plugins weren't built with performance as a priority.

The fix isn't to skip IDX — it's to build the listing and search experience around it deliberately, so it loads fast and looks like your site instead of a bolted-on widget.

## One page per neighborhood, not one page for your whole market

"Homes for sale in [your city]" is a different search — with different competitors and different intent — than "homes for sale in [specific neighborhood]." A single homepage trying to rank for your entire market dilutes relevance for all of them. Dedicated neighborhood pages, each genuinely useful (not just a filtered listing view), consistently outperform a single generic search page.

## The valuation funnel most agents skip

A "What's my home worth?" page is one of the highest-intent lead magnets available to an agent — it targets sellers actively curious about listing, at exactly the moment they're deciding whether to search for an agent. Yet a large share of agent sites either don't have one or bury it under a generic "Contact" link instead of making it a clear entry point.

## Speed matters more for real estate than almost any other industry

Listing photos are heavy. IDX widgets are heavy. Combined, they make real estate one of the slowest-loading site categories on the web — and buyers browsing listings on their phone between showings have zero patience for a slow gallery. A [2-second improvement in load time](/roi-calculator) can be the difference between a buyer requesting a showing and bouncing back to the search results.
