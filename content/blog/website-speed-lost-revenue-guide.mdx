If your website takes more than a couple of seconds to load on mobile, you're not just losing search rankings — you're losing customers who never even see your offer.

## The 2.5-second threshold that actually matters

Google's Core Web Vitals define a "good" Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) — the point where your main content becomes visible — as **2.5 seconds or faster**. Past that threshold, two things happen at once:

- Google treats your page as a worse experience, which can affect ranking.
- Real visitors start leaving before your page even finishes loading.

The second effect is the one most business owners underestimate.

## What each extra second actually costs

Industry benchmarks (Akamai, Portent, and Google's own research) consistently show that conversion rates drop roughly **7% for every additional second** of load time beyond that 2.5-second mark. It compounds fast:

- A site that loads in 4.5 seconds — 2 seconds over benchmark — can be losing **~14%** of the conversions it would otherwise get.
- At 6.5 seconds, that climbs toward **~28%**.

For a business running paid traffic or investing in SEO, that's not a rounding error. You're paying full price to acquire a visitor, then losing them for a reason that has nothing to do with your offer.

## Why this happens on hand-built and page-builder sites alike

Slow load times aren't unique to Wix or WordPress — a poorly optimized custom build can be just as slow. The usual culprits:

1. **Unoptimized images** served at their original resolution instead of resized for the display size.
2. **Render-blocking JavaScript** — third-party scripts (chat widgets, analytics, ad pixels) loaded before the page can paint.
3. **Client-side rendering the above-the-fold content**, so the browser has to download and run JavaScript before showing anything.
4. **Unnecessary font and CSS bloat** shipped on every page regardless of what's actually used.

## Fixing it

The good news: this is one of the more fixable problems in marketing. Unlike a soft market or a weak offer, page speed is almost entirely within your control — image sizing, script loading strategy, and font handling are all solvable in a rebuild without touching your brand or copy.
